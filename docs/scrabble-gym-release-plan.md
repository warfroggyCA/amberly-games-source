# Scrabble Gym — first release plan

**Status: implementation authorized, September 24, 2026. P0 baseline verified and P1 local preview implemented; P1 strategy feasibility has not passed. No migration or hosted deployment performed.** See the [build status and benchmark report](scrabble-gym-build-status.md). This plan implements the [training specification](scrabble-training-spec.md). Current-source observations refer to local branch `codex/home-play-animation`, commit `65ddd4a6498752c7fcaae6f763c04a94fa26c4bd`; refresh the baseline before implementation. Existing uncommitted documentation must be preserved.

**Release outcome.** A signed-in family member opens Scrabble Gym, receives a freshly generated legal two-player position and seven rack tiles, makes a move, learns how it compares for points and strategy, and can retrieve their saved practice history from their player profile on another device.

The agreed requirements are fresh random generation for every new puzzle; one simulated opponent; both score and strategy feedback; starting words animating from the playing positions; selecting a starting square, tapping it again to toggle across/down, then tapping or flicking rack tiles into successive destinations; a double-down-arrow Return all control and single-letter Undo, with dragging available for adjustments; a welcome/choice screen with How to play, a visible clue-based Hint button and Solve; profile-linked results across devices; online word lookup with confirmed additions shared across ordinary Scrabble and Gym for all family players/devices; and separation from ordinary family-game records. The name Scrabble Gym and Scarlett lifting Scrabble-themed weights remain the proposed branding. Easy, Medium and Hard are future modes whose definitions remain open. Unfinished tile-placement drafts remain local in version one.

**Recommended first-release defaults.** Use untimed mixed practice, the standard English tile set, the current pinned family reference, and early/middle-game situations. Offer unlimited learning retries while retaining the first attempt. Make personal Gym history visible to its owner by default. Include exchanges and pass as strategic choices. Keep full bot matches, multiplayer training, adaptive difficulty, competitions, daily challenges and cross-device draft handoff outside this release.

Additional refinements recommended in this plan are: Check move does not reveal an answer; Show me why is an explicit reveal; each revealed comparison teaches one supported lesson; the generator rejects unusable or uninformative situations; and a local diagnostic export can reproduce a questioned verdict. These are proposed implementation defaults, not new user-approved product requirements.

No release date is committed before P1 establishes the engine's speed, quality and any operating cost.

- Verify the pinned Node 24 environment, lockfile, private word artifact and its checksums. Do not replace the family reference with an example or external engine dictionary.

**P1 — prove the hard part with a small local prototype.**

Build a simple internal review screen and repeatable benchmark harness, without adding the public Gym entry yet. Fixed positions are regression fixtures only. The actual prototype must have a New random puzzle action that generates new state on every use.

1. Implement a deterministic seeded random source and a physical bag/rack model. The seed makes a generated puzzle reproducible; it does not select a preset board. Keep fresh seed generation separate from deterministic replay.
2. Generate synthetic legal two-player games from an empty board. Deal seven tiles to each player, apply varied legal placements, draw from the actual remaining bag and accumulate real scores. Capture a human-turn position after at least two placements, with seven human tiles and at least fourteen bag tiles.
3. Add a validator for the generation trace, current board, words, scores, rack counts, blanks and all 100 physical tiles. A board that looks plausible is insufficient. Bound turns, retries and work; failed generation remains a preparation failure.
4. Extend the existing enumeration machinery to obtain a histogram of all attainable placement scores, a count of maximum-score placements and representative answers. Evaluate arbitrary submitted actions against the original rack. Confirm ranking against the independent brute-force approach already used in `tests/solver.test.ts`.
5. Establish a baseline bot policy and strategy evaluator. Measure static rack evaluation, sampled replies and sufficiently deep continuation analysis separately. Do not use the current top-five immediate-score list as the entire strategy candidate set. Include promising lower-scoring plays, exchanges, pass and the actual user submission.
6. Evaluate strategies using only public state and the player's rack. Sample unseen racks and bag allocations; neither policy may inspect the other rack or future draws when choosing. Use paired sampled worlds, fresh verification samples for the selected recommendation, recorded budgets and explicit uncertainty.
7. Test the proposed expected-result metric from the spec. Full simulated outcomes or a separately validated terminal-value model must support it. Static score-plus-rack estimates are useful baselines, but do not become claimed winning odds. If a different strategy metric is necessary, document the evidence and proposed specification change before adopting it.
8. Compare an original implementation with an external engine adapter only if the original approach misses the quality or latency targets. Check compatibility with the exact custom word list, family exchange/end/tie rules, deployment target, engine license and any separate rack-value data. No package is selected in this plan.

Use 10–20 deliberately chosen fixtures covering ties, blanks, crosses, rack preservation, lower-score strategic preferences and score-context changes, plus at least 100 fresh random seeds for generation checks and desktop profiling. Exercise a representative set on the actual target iPhone; record sample counts before reporting percentiles. Human review checks both the recommendation and whether its explanation follows from the evidence. Broader candidate sets and increased budgets are comparison baselines, not proof of mathematical strategic optimality.

If caps require filtering, document which positions are excluded and check that the remaining mix still provides useful training. If the expected strategy coverage or phone budget cannot be met, stop UI expansion and resolve the engine or compute approach. Any hosted computation proposal must include estimated usage, cost, limits and privacy consequences. A preset bank or unlabelled heuristic is not a fallback.

The P1 deliverables are the local review screen, test fixtures, random-seed benchmark results, a comparison of score and strategy recommendations, a proposed engine/policy version and a recommendation to proceed or revise a specific assumption.

**P2 — stabilise the engine and its boundaries.**

Proposed new code lives in `src/domain/gym/`, with focused modules for types/validation, physical inventory, generation, grading and strategy. Keep pure game calculations separate from browser and server code. Reuse `board.ts`, `scoreMove`, lexicon resolution and existing placement enumeration. Add only the extensions needed for training; preserve ordinary scoring and assisted-finish behavior.

Define immutable puzzle input, canonical action identity, score analysis, strategy analysis, assistance events and attempt result contracts. A puzzle's reproducible record includes its generator version/seed, public position, full rules/reference identity and generation evidence. Its public-position fingerprint uses board tiles/blank flags, rack multiset, scores, bag/rack counts, turn, rules and the effective dictionary identity (base plus immutable accepted-additions hash); it excludes rack display order and seed so the same position cannot evade replay detection by shuffling or reseeding.

Include a compact public setup trace with each puzzle: generating seat, turn order and only the newly placed physical tiles for that turn. It supports the opening animation without exposing current hidden racks. Keep the generator's complete synthetic state behind a separate public-position projection. Only that projection crosses into the coach interface. This is an enforced function/data contract and regression test, not a promise that local personal practice is tamper-proof. Store the public snapshot for history; a seed alone is not sufficient recovery if generator code later changes.

The score analysis must count all unique physical placements and every score bucket after deduplication. Preserve the existing stable answer ordering only for display. A tie in points receives equal score rank. Store a bounded page of answer details plus a continuation mechanism when there are more maximum-score placements than can be shown at once.

Introduce a Gym worker/client protocol separate from the existing assisted-move worker. Include request ID, puzzle fingerprint, input revision and engine/reference version on requests and results. Support generation, exact score search, comparison of a submitted action and answer retrieval. Validate results before consuming them; wrong puzzle/version, malformed output, cancellation and budget exhaustion cannot become final results.

Use one active analysis worker initially, with active feedback ahead of one queued next-puzzle preparation. Benchmark before adding concurrency that might compete for a phone's CPU. Reuse a loaded lexicon within that worker, terminate on navigation/sign-out where needed, and clean up listeners and pending promises. Cancellation must actually stop work; merely posting a message to a synchronous busy worker is insufficient. Either search cooperatively yields or terminate/restart it while retaining the puzzle seed and draft.

Keep dictionary-correction lineage alongside the revision-specific fingerprint. A corrected puzzle retains its physical-position/session exposure identity, original generation reference and trace; changing its additions snapshot cannot grant a new first-attempt credit or claim that replaying the seed under a changed dictionary regenerates the same board.

P2 tests cover dictionary-revision cache invalidation and preserved exposure across corrections, repeated letters, two blanks, impossible racks, supply conservation after exchanges, used premiums, scoring ties, invalid crossed words, passes, terminal adjustments, stale results, cancellation and worker lifecycle. Include a test that changes only the generator's hidden rack/draw order while holding public state and evaluation seed fixed: coaching must not change.

**P3 — save owned results safely and sync them across devices.**

Use the existing server-mediated database pattern. `createAuthContext().requireUser()` verifies the account; the repository transaction checks active membership and the current linked `player_id`. The server chooses `family_id` and profile ownership. Client-supplied names, profile IDs, prior cookies or permission claims do not establish authority.

The current schema has composite player identity `(family_id, id)` and a unique membership-to-player link. Retain both. Use the restricted `scrabble_runtime` role, transaction-local actor settings and ownership policies in the existing `scrabble` schema. Reuse the existing ownership helper where its grants and behavior fit. Avoid generic family-wide read policies on personal Gym records. An administrator's broad roster access does not automatically expose another member's practice history. These choices follow the existing app model; [Supabase's RLS guidance](https://supabase.com/docs/guides/database/postgres/row-level-security) also requires grants and row policies to be considered together.

Proposed minimal schema, to finalise against the actual database before writing the migration:

| Table                   | Purpose and constraints                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                               |
| ----------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| `scrabble.gym_puzzles`  | Profile-owned immutable public snapshot, fingerprint, seed/generator/rules/reference metadata and validated score-analysis version. Composite foreign key to the existing player; unique public fingerprint per profile/reference scope. Bounded JSON payload.                                        |
| `scrabble.gym_sessions` | One encounter with a puzzle: stable ID, owner, puzzle reference, revision/next sequence, replay/exposure status and first-attempt projection. No copied player names as identity.                                                                                                                     |
| `scrabble.gym_events`   | Append-only hint, reveal, lookup/external-assistance, dictionary-correction, attempt, assessment and session-end events. Stable event ID and canonical payload hash; unique event ID per owner and unique session sequence. An assessment can arrive after the original attempt without rewriting it. |

Index owner plus server creation time and ID for stable cursor pagination, session plus sequence for replay, and the puzzle fingerprint for exposure lookup. Composite foreign keys prevent cross-family or cross-profile associations. Bound JSON shapes in code and essential constraints in SQL. Start with indexed queries over immutable records for summaries; add a separate aggregate table only if measurements justify it.

Store a generated snapshot when the member first interacts meaningfully with it: valid attempt, hint, Solve or an explicit session end. Coalesce snapshot/session creation with the first queued event so rejected/background candidates do not grow shared history. Resuming an already saved puzzle uses its snapshot; starting New puzzle generates fresh state.

Use `src/server/gym-repository.ts`, a factory in `src/server/database.ts`, a strict `src/lib/gym-contract.ts` and `/api/family/gym/route.ts`. Keep the Gym API separate from `describeSharedChange`, ordinary game commands and the existing superadmin-only `mode=practice`.

| API operation       | Required behavior                                                                                                                                                                                                            |
| ------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| GET summary/history | Own profile only; bounded cursor pagination; server totals plus versions/denominators. No full-family export as a shortcut.                                                                                                  |
| GET session detail  | Authorize the owner before returning the original puzzle, attempts, assistance and saved analysis. Viewing it is review, not a new unassisted attempt.                                                                       |
| POST event batch    | Bounded ordered events and optional initial snapshot; authenticate, validate, deduplicate and commit atomically. Start with at most 25 events and a 128 KiB request limit, then verify those limits against actual payloads. |
| POST assessment     | Append a completed or unavailable assessment for a specific saved attempt, preserving its original assistance state and engine version. Reuse event-batch semantics rather than inventing a second retry protocol.           |

Retain `readMutationJson` same-origin/content-type/body checks, private no-store JSON responses, cookie refresh behavior, the existing signed-in-user header check and safe repository errors. GETs have no write side effects. Test direct URL access and malformed IDs as well as normal UI calls. Rate-limit costly validation appropriately after measuring the family's workload; never perform long simulation under the family transaction lock.

**Reuse the shared verified-word catalog, with a Gym member entry point.** Current source already has `OfficialWordSearch`, `GET /api/official-word`, the exact-spelling publisher adapter, `extendLexicon` and `scrabble.verified_words` keyed by `(family_id, word)`. Ordinary shared `verify-words` independently verifies missing spellings, checks scorer permission, appends evidence and updates the active game's journal; new shared games load the catalog. Gym must use the same catalog without fabricating an ordinary game or relaxing that scorer permission. The existing lookup component's device-only saving description also needs context-specific copy for shared Gym use. These are source observations, not a deployed-behavior test.

Add a narrow authenticated **POST verify word** operation accepting a normalised spelling and idempotency key, independent of an ordinary game ID. Every active Gym member may invoke it. Authorise membership before publisher work and again in the short committing transaction. Reuse trusted stored evidence or obtain an exact positive verdict server-side; never accept a client-provided URL, timestamp or playable flag as authority. Keep fixed-origin fetching, input/response bounds, timeouts, parser validation and bounded request rates. Do not hold a database lock during a network request.

Insert only missing words with immutable evidence, audit the origin and return a receipt plus the effective catalog snapshot/hash. Enforce family isolation and retry/concurrency behavior through the existing restricted role and membership policies, checking the actual grants during implementation. A uniqueness conflict from another valid request returns the existing accepted entry. Verification success and persistence success are distinct; retries after a lost receipt must converge without duplicate additions. Reuse existing catalog size limits and return an actionable limit error rather than dropping words. A failure must preserve the draft and pending lookup state. General lookup GETs remain read-only.

New Gym puzzles include a validated snapshot of shared additions in generation, scoring, enumeration, coaching and saved provenance. Refresh the catalog on entry/resume and before admitting a newly prepared puzzle; invalidate or reanalyse a prefetched puzzle if its reference is stale. An active puzzle remains pinned until the explicit correction flow. A successful challenge derives a new immutable puzzle/reference revision for the same physical state and appends a correction event linking it to the original session. Preserve draft, attempts, help and exposure identity; cancel old analysis and regenerate comparisons/hints consistently. Dictionary persistence can succeed even when recomputation fails, so show those states independently. Existing ordinary-game journals and saved Gym assessments are never rewritten by this endpoint. Include the catalog and correction events in backup/recovery and sync acceptance.

**Stored result provenance must be explicit.** Independently validate a submitted position/trace, rack ownership and actual move score on the server. Complete deterministic enumeration once per saved puzzle/reference when marking its maximum/rank as server-verified. Measure this verification cost in P1 and keep it outside short membership/write locks, then recheck ownership inside the committing transaction. If verification is pending or exceeds its budget, retain the valid attempt and its status; do not discard the attempt or mark the rank verified. Cache only against the exact immutable puzzle/reference identity.

Strategy remains an estimate from the recorded evaluator. A bounded, schema-validated browser-engine result can be saved with that provenance; it is not certified by merely passing through the server. Do not trust a posted aggregate, success counter or free-form “best move” flag. Reject inconsistent scores/results and never promote client-reported estimates into family competition records. A later competitive feature would require stronger server-authoritative evaluation and is outside this plan.

**Sync protocol and ordering.** Write each local event to the IndexedDB outbox before acknowledging it as recoverable. Submit the same event ID and payload on retry. The server checks current ownership, then returns the original receipt for an identical event; the same ID with different content is a conflict. A lost response after commit cannot create a second attempt. Record both client occurrence time and server receipt time, but neither an editable client clock nor HTTP arrival order alone establishes first-attempt credit.

Per-session sequence numbers and expected revision preserve hint/reveal/attempt order; missing predecessors wait for retry. Two devices normally create separate sessions because live draft handoff is excluded. Both histories append without replacing each other. When the same fingerprint appears again, preserve prior exposure and label the session a replay. If offline sessions leave exposure order ambiguous, retain both and withhold duplicate unassisted credit rather than guessing. Grant at most one independent first-attempt credit for a profile/puzzle identity.

Show Saved to profile only after a successful server receipt. Waiting to sync retains the local event; Could not sync offers retry without clearing it. Retry on restored connectivity, focus/resume and explicit action with bounded backoff. Account changes and profile-link changes pause mismatched queues; never reattribute or automatically delete them. Cached screens and pending operations are keyed by family, authenticated account and player ID. Shared stored results remain attached to the stable profile; relinking authority remains governed by the existing account-link process.

Create an additive migration using the repository's current Supabase CLI workflow when implementation reaches this stage; do not invent a timestamp now. Register it in `config/database-migrations.json`. Add a Gym-specific schema capability check so a missing Gym migration disables Gym cleanly without breaking existing games. Preserve `application_schema_v2()` for current readers. Test migration on disposable copies, replay/recovery, runtime-role RLS and denial cases. No destructive down migration or history rewrite is required; rollback retains the Gym tables.

P3 acceptance also covers non-scorer Gym verification, another member/device using the accepted word in Gym and new ordinary games, forged evidence, family isolation, duplicate concurrent additions, lost receipts, catalog limits and a saved dictionary correction whose analysis fails. P3 acceptance includes two separate browser stores against the real disposable backend, a commit followed by a lost acknowledgement, reordered retry batches, conflicting payload IDs, simultaneous devices, unauthorized profile/family IDs, unlinked users, suspended membership and link changes. Verify row counts, event order and aggregate results directly; successful HTTP responses alone are insufficient.

The screen states are welcome/setup, preparing, introducing the board, ready, editing, checking, assessed, revealed and preparation/analysis unavailable. How to play and the clue panel are dismissible views that retain the active state and draft. Sync status is separate: waiting, syncing, saved or failed. A slow network must not erase a correct local result; a failed analysis must not become a failed player attempt.

**P5 — make profile progress useful and durable.**

Add My Gym progress from the practice entry and the signed-in player's own profile. Keep the existing family records UI intact. Summary figures come from the same authorised event history on every device, with pending local attempts shown separately until acknowledged.

- Show puzzles attempted, studied through a reveal, skipped and replayed separately.
- Show unassisted first-attempt maximum-score rate with its eligible-attempt denominator. Assisted attempts and later retries are separate learning results.
- Show average percentage of maximum points for eligible placement attempts; exchanges and passes do not enter that average.
- Show first-attempt strategy comparisons/gaps only where a compatible evaluator and sufficiently supported analysis exist. Do not average raw win estimates into a player-skill score or combine incompatible models without labelling the groups.
- Offer recent-history detail with the original board/rack, submitted action, help state and saved feedback. Keep unavailable old engine versions readable as recorded history, without silently regrading them.
- Store timestamps in UTC and format them for the viewing user's locale/time zone. Use stable server-time-plus-ID pagination. Defer streaks and daily awards so timezone boundaries do not become a release dependency.

Record replay and assistance events in a way that neither refresh nor another device can erase prior help. For a later improved analysis, append its version and show that it is a new assessment; retain the original attempt result. Exclude verification-pending/unavailable values from success percentages and show their count.

A small Report a problem action can offer a local JSON download containing the puzzle snapshot/seed, relevant attempt, rules/reference/evaluator versions and error category. Exclude auth tokens, private account details and unrelated history. Nothing is sent automatically. Reuse safe diagnostics conventions and add only Gym failure categories needed to diagnose generation, analysis and sync failures.

Measure history-query cost with enough synthetic results to exercise pagination and indexes; check query plans and avoid one query per row. Verify the existing schema-wide backup tool captures and restores all new tables, indexes/policies and row fingerprints. A cleared browser cache must still allow synced history to load on a fresh device.

**P6 — integrate the design and Scarlett without obstructing practice.**

Match Amberly's existing board, tile, typography and navigation conventions. Keep the rack and primary action reachable in phone portrait and landscape. Normal play selects a start square once and uses rack taps/clicks or flicks for successive placements, without summoning the software keyboard. Repeated taps on the selected square toggle across/down; show an accessible direction arrow/label and an obvious next destination. Keep ↶ Undo and ⇊ Return all beside the rack with visible text and accessible names. Separate fixed tiles, new tiles and answer overlays using more than colour. Support screen-reader square/rack labels, keyboard-operated tile movement, direction changes and focus restoration. Dragging remains available for adjustments.

Build the opening board animation into P4's functional flow, then tune it here. Inspect `TurnAnimation.tsx`, `AnimatedBoardEditor.tsx` and `turn-playback.ts` for reusable seat-origin geometry and motion. The current playback detects newly committed ordinary-game turns, so do not fabricate shared game events to drive Gym's intro. Use a display-only adapter for the validated public setup trace. Never move an already placed crossing letter twice or borrow tiles from the player's current rack. Interruptions and skips settle to the frozen final board; resume/history/retry do not repeat the intro. Include its duration in measured time-to-play.

Build one placement controller for tap, flick, drag and keyboard, with stable physical rack-tile IDs, a selected start, direction, insertion cursor and placement order. Tap/flick targets the shown cursor, advances past occupied squares along the chosen direction, and stops at the board boundary without wrapping or guessing a word. Retargeting/toggling affects future placements only. Default Across is a proposed interaction choice to verify on phones. Persist the cursor/direction and placement order with the local draft so Resume and Undo remain predictable.

## Strategy latency revision — September 24, 2026

The foreground full-game experiment did not meet its 15-second budget. The next local implementation tests a **short-horizon coaching estimate**, separately from expected game outcome: immediate points minus the opponent's sampled reply points, plus the difference in retained/drawn rack heuristics. This is not win probability, a calibrated player grade or an exact all-move strategy rank. Original full-continuation code remains a research baseline. Near-endgame positions require a dedicated evaluator and are excluded from this new midgame estimate.

Use the existing score and rack-preserving shortlist, legal exchanges, pass and the user's actual move. Every shortlisted candidate receives the same discovery racks. Freeze the discovery recommendation and compare it with the user's move on fresh paired racks. Show the actual move score, retained tiles, average reply score, sample variation and shortlist coverage. Do not silently average only completed samples. Benchmark completion rates before exposing the replacement; retain cancellation and draft preservation. This follows the truncated-lookahead approach described in [Macondo's primary technical documentation](https://domino14.github.io/macondo/howitworks.html), but our small sample budget and hand-written rack values do not inherit that engine's strength or calibration.
