# Scrabble Gym — practice and training

**Status: implementation authorized; local P1 engine preview built, full release incomplete.** See the [build status](scrabble-gym-build-status.md) for implemented features, measured limits and outstanding work. Written September 23, 2026; updated September 24 to require fresh random generation, profile-linked progress across devices, an animated board setup, tap/flick tile placement with a direction toggle, action-based Undo and Return all, and capture future difficulty modes. Source observations were made against local source `65ddd4a6498752c7fcaae6f763c04a94fa26c4bd` on `codex/home-play-animation` and do not establish the deployed state.

The [first release plan](scrabble-gym-release-plan.md) maps this specification to implementation stages, data/API contracts, verification and rollout. Additional product refinements are marked as recommendations there.

**The goal is to help a player find better moves and understand why they are better.** Each puzzle starts partway through a legal Scrabble game, with words already on the board and seven tiles on the player's rack. The player makes one decision, receives score and strategy feedback, studies the alternatives, and chooses when to start a new puzzle.

The user has confirmed two-player situations, with one simulated opponent, and practice results saved against the player profile and retrievable on any signed-in device in the first release. This replaces the earlier device-only progress decision. Both immediate score and strategy are required. Online word lookup must be available, and positively verified missing words must join the shared game dictionary for all family players and devices, across ordinary Scrabble and Gym. Gym opens with a choice screen and How to play, and includes a visible Hint button that provides clues. Starting words animate onto the board from the two playing positions, and the primary interaction is to select a starting square, toggle across/down by tapping it again, then tap or flick actual rack tiles into successive destinations. A double-down-arrow Return all control returns the draft tiles to the rack, and Undo reverses the last editing action. Dragging remains available for individual adjustments. Each new puzzle must be freshly generated at random, rather than selected from a preset bank. Easy, Medium and Hard modes are a captured future feature; their definitions remain open. Other choices below are proposed product defaults, not a claim that implementation or release has been approved.

**Scrabble Gym is the working name.** The user suggested Scarlett, the family dog, as an animated mascot lifting Scrabble-themed weights. Preserve this as a visual concept to develop: for example, Scarlett lifting a small barbell with letter-tile weights on the welcome screen or between puzzles. Confirm Scarlett's appearance from a suitable reference before creating the asset. The final illustration, animation and name remain design choices for review. Keep the board clear during play, and provide a still pose for reduced-motion settings. The name and mascot do not change the training requirements.

**The first version is a relaxed, untimed trainer.** It sits alongside the existing family Scrabble scorer. Active family members can use it without becoming a scorer or superadmin. It uses the standard 100-tile English set and the app's pinned family word reference. Custom equipment, three- and four-player situations, timed challenges, shared leaderboards and full games against a bot are later work.

The visible opponent provides game context; the player does not wait through a live bot turn. Background analysis considers possible future play. A two-player puzzle needs one opponent; a four-player version would need three opponents and a different evaluator.

**Start with a welcome and choice screen.** Opening Gym shows the linked player, available practice choices, **How to play**, **Start practice**, **Resume** when a local puzzle is unfinished, and **My progress**. It does not immediately open a board or replace an unfinished puzzle. Scarlett's proposed welcome animation belongs here.

**Updated after local-preview feedback:** use one combined practice mode with separate Score and Strategy assessments. Immediate points are exact; strategy estimates must show their uncertainty and availability. Do not make players select between two apparently different games. Keep a clear Back to Gym control. In the unsaved local preview, confirm before discarding an active puzzle; the full release retains the draft under its recovery contract. The welcome screen can later house Easy, Medium and Hard once their behavior is defined.

Changing settings applies to the next new puzzle. Resume retains the current puzzle and its recorded settings/help history. If starting afresh would abandon a draft, keep it recoverable or explicitly confirm that choice. Remember the most recent choice on the device; synchronising preferences is separate from the required profile-result sync. Opening the welcome screen may load shared assets, but begins no recorded attempt or puzzle introduction until Start or Resume.

**How to play explains the loop in a short, optional guide.** Make it available before starting and from the practice menu without losing the current draft. Cover these points:

1. A random legal position appears, and its setup tiles arrive from the playing positions. You have seven tiles and one move to improve the situation.
2. Tap an empty starting square; tap it again to toggle across/down. Then tap or flick rack tiles: each goes to the highlighted next square. Existing letters are skipped automatically. Use Undo for the last editing action or ⇊ Return all for the whole draft. Connect to existing words; all new words must be valid. A blank can represent any selected letter and is worth zero.
3. Select Check move to see points, score rank and an estimated strategy assessment. The highest-scoring move and the coach's choice may differ.
4. Hint gives progressively more specific clues. Solve reveals the answer and explanation. You may try again or choose Next puzzle.
5. Practice results are saved to your profile; help is recorded so independent and assisted attempts remain distinct. Waiting to sync means another device may not yet have the result.

The guide is not a forced tutorial and contains no answer-specific information. Opening it does not count as using a hint. Include drag adjustments, keyboard controls and a link to the fuller scoring explanation without overloading the first screen.

**The player journey keeps the lesson visible.**

1. Open **Scrabble → Scrabble Gym**, with **Practice and training** as the explanatory label. Use the welcome screen to read How to play, then select Start practice or Resume in one combined points-and-strategy mode. Show the linked player profile and whether results are saved to the profile or waiting to sync. Profile history is available on every signed-in device; unfinished placement drafts remain local in the first release.
2. Introduce the generated board by animating its setup tiles from the corresponding playing positions. Then present seven rack tiles, both players' scores, whose turn it is, the opponent's rack count and the number of tiles in the bag. It is always the human player's turn. The opponent's letters remain hidden.
3. Tap an empty starting square, tap it again if needed to toggle across/down, then tap or flick the desired rack tiles into the advancing highlighted destination. Select **Check move** when finished. Mouse clicks follow the same flow. Dragging individual tiles and keyboard-operated tile movement remain available; normal play requires no typing or software keyboard. Keep rack reorder/shuffle, **↶ Undo** for the last editing action and **⇊ Return all** within reach. These actions do not draw new letters.
4. Check legality and rack ownership. An invalid move remains editable, with a specific reason and affected squares or words. It does not consume the scored attempt.
5. Show two results: **Score** and **Strategy**. Keep the attempted placement on the original board. Do not refill the rack, commit a family-game turn or automatically start another puzzle.
6. Offer **Try again**, **Hint**, **Solve** and **Next puzzle**. Trying again restores the original board and rack; the first valid attempt remains recorded separately from later exploration.
7. **Next puzzle** loads a different position and rack. If the player has not submitted or revealed anything, record a skip. Leaving Practice preserves the current puzzle and draft.

Show the draft point total beside the placed word, green with a check when valid. Invalid words may show potential points in red with an explicit invalid label; structurally unscorable placements show an invalid marker without invented points. Before checking a move, do not show the maximum, the score gap, answer counts, suggested words or strategy verdicts unless the player requests help. A rejected word offers an explicit online check and, when verified as playable, a shared dictionary addition through the workflow below.

**The puzzle arrives from the playing positions.** On each newly generated puzzle, replay the public setup placements from the human and bot seat positions onto their final squares, using the generating turns to identify the correct origin. Animate only the physical tiles added by each setup turn: existing crossing letters must not fly in again. Unfilled destinations show the normal empty board until their tiles arrive, without brown placeholders. This is a brief introduction to the already validated position; it reveals no current hidden opponent rack or recommended solution.

Keep a compact public setup-animation trace with the puzzle: seat, turn order and newly placed tile coordinates/blank flags. The animation presents the frozen starting board and scores; it does not create real game turns, consume the player's current rack, change practice statistics or gate a save on an animation callback. Reuse the existing game's seat-origin motion conventions where practical. Provide Skip animation and a reduced-motion path directly to the finished board. Resume, history review, Try again and score/solution overlays do not replay the introduction automatically.

An interrupted animation, orientation change or failed animation API settles to the same complete starting position. Prevent placement while setup is moving; enable it immediately when the intro finishes or is skipped. Count the introduction in the total time before the player can act, so it stays brief and does not hide slow puzzle preparation.

**Select the start once, then tap or flick letters into place.** Tapping an empty square sets the insertion point. Show an arrow and an Across/Down label immediately; Across is the proposed initial default. Each further tap on that selected square toggles across/down, with no double-tap timing requirement. Also allow activating the direction arrow by touch, mouse or keyboard. This resolves ambiguous starting positions without guessing the intended word or suggesting an answer.

A tap on a rack tile, or a short flick toward the board, sends that physical tile to the highlighted insertion square. The flick does not need precise aim: the visible cursor determines its destination. Advance to the next empty square along the chosen direction, skipping fixed letters and already placed draft tiles without consuming more rack tiles. Outline the destination and show the direction with more than colour; keep the next destination visible at the current zoom. Stop at the board edge, never wrap to another row/column or silently change direction. With no selected start or no remaining destination, retain the tile in the rack and explain what to select.

Selecting another empty square moves the cursor and retains the direction. Tapping that selected square again toggles direction. These actions affect future placements only; they never rearrange existing draft tiles. The draft can be temporarily incomplete while editing; Check move validates its full alignment, connectivity and words. A blank opens its letter picker before consuming the tile or advancing the cursor; cancelling leaves the prior state intact. Brief tile movement makes the action readable, with an immediate/reduced-motion equivalent. Rapid taps on different rack tiles use successive destinations in input order, even while earlier animations finish; one gesture cannot consume the same physical tile twice.

**Keep action-based Undo and Return all beside the rack.** **↶ Undo** reverses the last completed edit: placement, relocation, individual return, Return all, rack shuffle or an explicit direction change. Moving a tile is undone by restoring its previous square. Mere square selection does not consume an undo step. Restore physical tile IDs, blank assignments, rack order and cursor/direction from the prior draft; never change fixed tiles, recorded attempts or assistance history. **⇊ Return all** returns all draft tiles in one undoable action. Disable Undo when editing history is empty, and disable Return all when no draft tiles remain. Block editing during attempt commitment. Dragging hides the source copy while showing the moving tile; cancelled or invalid drops restore it. Controls use compact icons with accessible names and tooltips.

**Dragging remains available for individual adjustments.** Show a lifted tile above the finger or pointer with its destination highlighted. A valid drop snaps that owned tile into the empty square; draft tiles can be moved or returned to the rack. An invalid drop or cancelled gesture restores its prior position. A successful placement or move updates the visible cursor consistently with its destination and the selected direction. Distinguish tap, flick, deliberate drag/reorder, board pan, pinch/zoom and page scroll so one gesture produces at most one placement. Rotation, lost pointer capture or sleep restores the last completed action; animation callbacks never decide tile ownership. Use the same placement controller and owned-tile validation for tap, flick, drag and keyboard. Keep fixed starting tiles immovable and preserve exact rack multiplicities, including repeated letters and blanks.

**Let the player challenge a missing word without losing the move.** Show **Check this word online** beside each unrecognised word, prefilled from the actual placement, including a rejected crossing word. Also offer **Word lookup** from the practice menu for an exact spelling. Distinguish missing vocabulary from an illegal placement, an unavailable dictionary and an analysis failure; only missing vocabulary is resolved by adding a word. Keep the draft, rack order, cursor and direction intact while the check runs or closes. A lookup field may use typing; entering a move on the board still requires none.

Use the existing Merriam-Webster Scrabble finder integration. Confirmation means the exact spelling is explicitly marked playable, as on its [word-check page](https://scrabble.merriam.com/finder/onyx). A general dictionary definition, search result, suggested anagram or model opinion is insufficient. Display the spelling, verdict, source link and verification date. Timeout, offline access or an unrecognised response means **Could not verify**, not a rejected word. Do not remove an already approved family word because an external source disagrees.

A positive check automatically saves a missing word through the server to the shared verified-word catalog, without an additional admin approval step. Here, globally means throughout this family's Amberly Scrabble dictionary: every player and signed-in device, ordinary games and Gym. Preserve the existing family boundary. Retain source evidence and a stable catalog identity; repeat checks or simultaneous additions save one word. Show **Added to the shared dictionary** only after the server confirms the write. A verified but unsaved word remains visibly pending with retry; do not claim other devices have received it. New games and puzzles load the accepted shared additions. Other devices refresh the catalog on entry/resume; active games incorporate additions through their existing audited word-reference flow, without changing earlier turns.

**A confirmed addition updates the current puzzle through an explicit correction.** Preserve the generated board, rack, scores and draft; create a new immutable effective-reference revision containing the accepted additions. Recheck all words formed by the move, then show its points if it is legal. The player still uses Check move to submit it. Discard old worker replies and recalculate the exact score distribution, maximum, strategy comparisons and hint targets against that same revision before showing them. During recalculation, show points when known and **Updating comparisons**; a timeout leaves rankings/strategy unavailable. One accepted word does not make an invalid crossing word or disconnected placement legal.

Keep the original puzzle/reference and any saved assessments readable. Append the correction and any revised assessment rather than rewriting history. Carry forward the session's hint/reveal exposure and first-attempt identity across revisions, so a dictionary correction cannot create extra independent successes. A draft rejected only because the dictionary lacked its word has not used the first valid attempt. New hints use the updated analysis and identify the reference change; previous hints remain recorded as prior help. Related-word suggestions are not part of the exact lookup. Record lookup use separately from hints; the proposed default treats checking the player's own spelling as validation without a hint penalty. Opening a source page that exposes related words is recorded as external assistance for subsequent attempts.

**Score feedback is exact within the puzzle's word reference.** Rate the whole turn, including every word formed, premiums and the seven-tile bonus. Existing blanks stay worth zero and retain their assigned letters. Used premiums do not reactivate. These mechanics follow the existing scorer and [Hasbro's game guide](https://www.hasbro.com/common/documents/dad2884b1c4311ddbd0b0800200c9a66/0606acdbbc9d4b55bfd2df956f61a723.pdf).

| Result               | Definition                                                                                                                       |
| -------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Your score           | Points earned by this legal placement.                                                                                           |
| Maximum score        | Highest whole-turn score among all legal placements for this board and rack.                                                     |
| Points below maximum | Maximum score minus the submitted score.                                                                                         |
| Score rank           | `1 + number of legal placements scoring more than yours`. Use competition rank with explicit ties and the total placement count. |
| Ties                 | Every placement with the same score has the same score rank. Every maximum-scoring placement receives full score credit.         |
| Score percentage     | `100 × your score / maximum score`, rounded for display only. This measures point capture, not strategy.                         |

For attainable placement scores of 62, 62, 58 and 46, a 46-point play earns **#4 of 4 placements**, 16 points below maximum. Both 62-point plays earn **Maximum score**. Detailed results may also show the number of legal placements and how many achieve the maximum.

A placement is identified by its newly occupied squares, assigned letters and physical blank choices. The same word in a different location is a different placement. Swapping identical rack tiles is not. A single tile forming two words is one move. Assigning a blank to a different square can change the score and must remain a distinct move.

Exact ranks and maxima require a completed enumeration. A timeout, truncated search, missing reference or disagreement with the answer data must never produce a claimed maximum, exact rank or zero-move verdict. Retain the player's work and show that analysis is unavailable. Puzzle admission requires a positive maximum, so score percentages cannot divide by zero.

**Strategy feedback estimates the quality of the decision, given what the player knows.** Highest immediate score and best strategic choice can differ. The coach considers remaining rack tiles, likely draws and replies, board openings, score difference and game stage. Established engines distinguish static rack evaluation from simulation; this is a useful design precedent, not evidence that Amberly already has that capability. See [Quackle's explanation](https://people.csail.mit.edu/jasonkb/quackle/doc/how_quackle_plays_scrabble.html).

The proposed numeric measure is an **estimated game result**, derived from simulated continuations under a named, versioned bot policy:

`estimated result = 100 × (wins + 0.5 × draws) / completed simulations`

This is a model estimate on a 0–100 scale: a simulated win earns 100, an unresolved tie after the rules' tiebreak earns 50, and a loss earns zero. It is not an exact winning probability or a general player-skill score. Compare the player's estimate with the coach's recommended action from the same position. Keep the raw estimate in the detail view; make the main lesson the difference between the choices.

| Strategy result         | Proposed display rule                                                                                    |
| ----------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Coach's choice          | The submitted action matches the recommended action or a supported equivalent.                           |
| Competitive choice      | The estimated gap is at most 2 points on the 0–100 scale and the comparison is sufficiently precise.     |
| Better option available | A gap above 2 points is supported by the comparison's uncertainty interval.                              |
| Too close to call       | The analysis cannot reliably distinguish the choices or decide which side of the threshold they fall on. |
| Analysis unavailable    | Required analysis is incomplete, incompatible or failed. No strategy grade is invented.                  |

The 2-point threshold is a proposed product tolerance to validate during the engine experiment. Narrow simulation intervals describe sampling uncertainty, not certainty that the bot model matches human play. Show the evaluator version and assumptions in expandable details. Do not assign an exact “Xth-best strategy” rank.

Illustrative feedback, not a measured puzzle: **“46 points — 3rd-highest score. Competitive strategic choice. The coach prefers a 44-point move because it keeps a more useful rack. The estimated difference is small.”** If the highest-scoring play is strategically weaker, say so without downgrading its exact score achievement.

**The strategy engine must not see through the opponent's rack.** The evaluator receives the public board and scores, the player's rack, rack counts, bag count and the pinned rules/reference. Calculate unseen tiles as the full set minus the board and the player's rack. Sample the opponent's rack and bag from that pool without replacement. Do not remove the generator's secret opponent rack before sampling.

The first version assumes unseen allocations are random given that visible state; it does not infer rack contents from earlier decisions. The original hidden rack and future draw order are not inputs to the coach and are not shipped as answers. Each simulated actor chooses using its own rack and public information, without inspecting the other rack or future draws. After sampling, conserve that world's physical tiles throughout the continuation.

Evaluate candidates on shared sampled starting worlds where practical, with deterministic seeds and fair allocation of analysis effort. Check the selected recommendation with fresh evaluation samples, so choosing a lucky result from many candidates does not create false confidence. A simulation reaching a resource limit is incomplete; do not drop difficult continuations and average only the easy ones. Full continuations must reach a valid ending. A bounded lookahead may replace them only after a calibrated terminal-value model is established and explicitly versioned.

The engine's action space includes every unique legal placement, every legal exchange subset and pass. A candidate shortlist may help allocate simulation effort, but it cannot be only the five highest-scoring moves. Lower-scoring plays that preserve a good rack must be considered. Every legal action the user can submit needs its own supported assessment, including a poor action outside the eventual shortlist. Recommendations remain **best found under this model**, not proven optimal play. Compare shortlist results with wider searches during validation.

Exchanges and passes live under **Other moves**. They earn zero immediate points and no placement score rank, but receive strategy feedback. Otherwise the trainer could penalize a sound decision simply because it did not put down a word. Exchange availability and draw/return ordering follow the pinned training rules.

**Explanations must identify the actual trade-off.** Give one or two concise reasons, with board highlights or rack comparisons that support them. Examples include preserving complementary rack letters, making multiple crosswords, using a premium, reducing an opponent's high-scoring replies, or keeping opportunities open when behind. Describe sampled replies as possibilities, never predictions about the opponent's actual letters.

Use deterministic explanation templates backed by computed facts. Do not award a strategy score from an LLM opinion or invent a causal explanation from the chosen word alone. A current engine precedent also evaluates the user's actual move and explicitly handles indistinguishable alternatives: [Macondo's comparison approach](https://domino14.github.io/macondo/manual/ai-explainability.html). Definitions are optional supporting content; a failed definition lookup must not block feedback.

**A visible Hint button gives clues without immediately revealing the answer.** Keep Hint beside the main play actions so it is available before the first attempt and while trying again. The player can request **Improve score** or **Improve strategy**, initially matching the chosen practice focus. Each hint sequence follows one consistent target action; it must not switch between tied solutions and become contradictory. Show the current clue and **Another hint**, with progress such as “Hint 1 of 3.” Solve remains a separate deliberate action; exhausting clues never automatically reveals the complete answer.

Examples of clue wording are “Look for a way to score in two directions” or “Consider which letters you will keep.” Use them only when supported by the actual target's evidence. More specific clues can highlight a region, give a tile count or reveal a starting square. Preserve the draft when opening or closing the clue panel. Record only a successfully revealed clue as assistance; a failed request does not consume a hint. Prevent repeated taps from skipping clue levels.

1. Give a relevant concept, such as considering the rack left behind or looking for a parallel play.
2. Narrow the search to a board area or the number of tiles to use. For exchanges, identify the rack problem rather than a board square.
3. Reveal a start square/direction or part of the action. For exchanges, reveal part of the suggested tile selection.
4. **Solve** reveals the complete action and explanation.

Show that help has been used, with no point penalty. Hint use before the first valid attempt makes that attempt assisted. Help requested after the first attempt does not retroactively change its status. Shuffle, placement undo and clearing the draft are not hints. Exact-word lookup and external source visits use the separate assistance rules above.

**Solve shows both kinds of answer.** Present **Highest score** and **Coach's recommendation**. If they are the same move, say so. If they differ, explain the trade-off. Let the player switch among their move, the maximum-score move and the recommended move as overlays on the unchanged starting board. Show actual scoring arithmetic, crosswords, blank assignments and the rack left behind. When there are several maximum-score placements, show the count and allow inspection of alternatives without pretending a five-item list is exhaustive.

Reveal without an attempt is recorded as studied, not failed or solved independently. After a reveal, the player may replay the placement to learn it, but that replay cannot become a new unassisted result.

**Every new puzzle is freshly generated at random.** Generate synthetic two-player games from an empty board using randomized legal turns, actual racks, a finite bag, correct draws and scores. Capture positions after at least two placements, while it is the human seat's turn and that seat has exactly seven tiles. Retain a generation seed, generator version and record that can reproduce and validate the position. Do not mine private family-game history or select new puzzles from a preset bank. Fixed positions are development and regression fixtures only; saved positions support resuming work and reviewing earlier attempts.

The proposed initial generator targets early and middle-game positions with at least 14 bag tiles; late-game training can follow. Every presented puzzle must have at least two legal placements and a positive maximum. Generate varied opportunities for hooks, parallel plays, premiums, rack management and physical blanks, including positions where score and strategy agree and positions where they differ. Avoid recent duplicate board/rack positions using content fingerprints. Random generation does not guarantee that a position can never recur, but New puzzle must not deliberately replay a stored answer. Do not make every puzzle a bingo or require obscure vocabulary for every recommended move.

**Future feature: Easy, Medium and Hard.** The user suggested fewer solutions and rarer solution words as possible Hard-mode characteristics. Preserve these as candidates to explore, not settled definitions or extra first-release requirements. Possible starting points for later design are:

- **Easy:** familiar solution words, several strong available moves and easier-to-spot scoring opportunities.
- **Medium:** less obvious placements, more crossing constraints and clearer trade-offs between immediate points and the rack left behind.
- **Hard:** fewer strong or near-best moves, rarer solution words, subtle hooks or parallel plays, and harder strategic trade-offs.

Measure total legal placements separately from the number of strong or near-best choices. Fewer total placements may make a puzzle easier to search, so that count alone cannot determine difficulty. Word rarity also needs an agreed measure and source; dictionary membership does not establish familiarity. Validate difficulty with actual play before setting thresholds. Keep vocabulary difficulty and strategic difficulty distinguishable so Hard does not simply mean obscure words.

All three modes must still generate fresh random positions. A future selected level guides generation and acceptance filters, without changing word legality, scoring rules or the meaning of the feedback. The first release remains mixed practice, with lesson and complexity metadata available for later tuning. Focused drills, adaptive selection and repeat-mistake scheduling are also later additions.

Before presenting a generated puzzle, replay the generation history, verify all words and connectivity, reconcile every physical tile, check scores/rack/bag counts and complete the exact score search. Strategy analysis may continue in the background, with a visible analysing state when needed. Reject malformed or unsolved positions. Bound generation retries and analysis work; exhaustion produces a retryable preparation failure, not a preset fallback. Use varied generation choices rather than having an always-greedy bot create every board.

**Runtime generation and analysis are required.** Prepare one fresh random puzzle ahead while the player works on the current one. This temporary preparation is not selection from an authored puzzle library. Keep generation and search off the main UI thread and cancel obsolete work. Prioritise the active puzzle's feedback over preparing the next one. Cache completed analysis for that puzzle and assess the user's actual action on demand if it was not already analysed. Never mistake a candidate shortlist for coverage of every possible submission.

The first engineering experiment must prove that fresh generation, exact score enumeration and supported strategy feedback are practical within measured time, memory and battery budgets. Start with a local prototype and use fixed test fixtures only to verify correctness. If on-device analysis is too slow, evaluate explicit compute alternatives and waiting behavior; do not silently replace random generation with preset content. A score-only intermediate build is an engineering milestone; it does not complete the requested feature. No engine package or new hosted compute service is selected by this document.

**Each puzzle and attempt has a stable identity.** The implementation needs the following logical records; these are data requirements, not a prescribed database schema.

| Record          | Required contents                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                      |
| --------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Puzzle          | ID and content hash; generation seed; schema and generator version; original board with blank flags; ordered rack; both scores; turn and rack counts; bag count; tile-set snapshot; rules version; exact base lexicon ID, edition and checksum; immutable accepted-additions snapshot/hash and any correction lineage. |
| Score answer    | Solver version; completed status; count of unique placements; histogram of all attainable scores; maximum; count and identities of maximum-score placements.                                                                                                                                                           |
| Strategy answer | Evaluator and bot-policy versions; sampling assumptions and seed; action identity; estimate and uncertainty; sample counts; evidence for explanations; coverage and recommendation status.                                                                                                                             |
| Attempt         | Stable attempt ID; family and player-profile IDs; authenticated actor; session ID; puzzle identity; canonical placement or exchange/pass action; feedback versions; exact points/rank where applicable; strategy result; hint/reveal state at submission; first/later attempt marker; timestamp.                       |
| Local workspace | Current puzzle, draft, rack order, placement order, selected start, insertion cursor/direction, current overlay, hints/reveal status, completed-attempt references and schema version, namespaced by signed-in family/account.                                                                                         |

The initial reference is `amberly-family-v1-2121ea84c411`, the 176,974-word custom family list. It is not labelled an official NWL or OSPD edition. Pin the base and accepted-additions snapshot when generating a puzzle. An explicit verified-word correction may create a new reference revision for the same position through the workflow above; never silently replace the original reference. New dictionaries, evaluator changes or corrections produce new versioned answers; earlier results retain their original meaning. Retain compatible old puzzle data for saved work, or mark it unavailable while preserving the recorded result.

The rules snapshot also fixes exchange legality, ending conditions and tie resolution. The current scorer permits exchanges when the bag contains at least the number exchanged; its blocked ending requires each player to pass twice consecutively, and it uses pre-adjustment scores to break final-score ties where possible. Training simulations must implement their pinned rules explicitly, rather than silently importing different tournament-engine defaults.

**Practice success belongs to the player profile and is available across devices.** Persist completed attempts, assistance/reveal history and progress against the existing stable player-profile ID in shared storage. Signing in on another device retrieves the same saved history and summaries from the profile’s Gym view. Retain the puzzle snapshot and pinned analysis references needed to review each recorded attempt; do not regenerate a different board for a history entry. Keep training data separate from ordinary family-game records. Show puzzles attempted/studied, unassisted maximum-score results, assisted results, and average first-attempt strategy gap where the analysis supports comparison. Score-percentage averages apply only to placement attempts. Exclude skips, reveals without attempts and unavailable assessments from performance averages; show their counts separately.

Treat retries as learning, not extra independent successes. A repeated puzzle is labelled a replay and cannot erase its original first-attempt/help record. Do not combine results across incompatible evaluator/reference versions without identifying the difference. These are practice summaries, not a calibrated Scrabble rating or a family leaderboard.

Use the existing verified account-to-player link for ownership. The server resolves and authorizes that link on every read and write; a client-supplied profile ID or matching display name is not sufficient. A member without a linked profile must complete the existing linking/onboarding flow before saving profile results. Do not create duplicate profiles or permit arbitrary profile selection. Proposed default visibility is the player’s own practice history; saving against a profile does not automatically publish it to other family members or a leaderboard.

Retain local IndexedDB storage for the current draft, cached history and a queue of unsynced attempts, scoped to family, account and player profile. Use explicit **Saved to profile**, **Waiting to sync** and **Could not sync** states. Only a server acknowledgement means the result is available on another device. Clearing browser data does not remove already-synced history, but can lose pending local work. Unfinished draft handoff between devices is not required for the first release; saved results and review history are.

Retries must submit the same attempt ID and payload. Server writes atomically persist the attempt and its assistance state, and either derive summaries from those records or update them transactionally. The same attempt arriving twice counts once; legitimate different attempts from two devices are both retained. Use stable session/puzzle identity and enforce first-attempt credit at that scope, without trusting device timestamps to establish order. Version checks prevent stale overwrites; never replace a profile’s whole history with a device’s older snapshot. Refresh saved history on sign-in, focus/resume and successful sync.

Namespacing and account switching must prevent one member’s cached progress appearing in another member’s UI. Sign-out clears the active in-memory workspace. Never upload a pending attempt as a different signed-in player. Membership revocation or a changed account-to-profile link pauses affected uploads for resolution; it does not reassign or discard them. A local storage failure preserves current work in memory and states that recovery is unavailable. A shared-save failure retains the pending attempt for retry. Any future clear-progress action must distinguish clearing this device’s cache from deleting shared profile history and require confirmation for the latter.

**The trainer must preserve work through interruptions.** Save a recoverable draft after meaningful changes. Check move, Solve and Next must be protected against double activation, with stable attempt IDs and puzzle IDs. Ignore late responses for an old puzzle. Coordinate two tabs so a stale tab cannot overwrite newer work or inflate statistics. Keep the current review until the next puzzle is successfully loaded.

Use explicit loading, ready, draft, checking, feedback, revealed and unavailable states. “Try again” resets only the placement, not assistance or attempt history. A generated, fetched or cached answer must pass schema, version and content checks before use. If a valid submitted action is missing from a supposedly complete answer set, report an analysis defect instead of grading it as bad.

Once a puzzle and its required local assets and analysis are ready, network loss should not interrupt that puzzle. Initial offline sign-in and fresh generation that depends on any future remote compute are not promised. Incomplete remote analysis must remain unavailable while offline. Do not substitute an unverified puzzle or different dictionary when preparation fails.

Mobile acceptance includes portrait and landscape, reliable tap/flick placement and repeated-tap direction switching, an unobscured next destination, accurate drag adjustments, zoom/pan that does not conflict with tile movement, reachable Undo/Return all and rack controls, clear distinction between fixed and draft tiles, and an explicit letter picker for blanks. Normal play must not invoke the software keyboard; keyboard accessibility and any other focused inputs must still preserve the viewport. Do not rely on colour or dragging alone. Include keyboard navigation, meaningful square/rack labels, announced errors and results, adequate touch targets, and reduced-motion support. Physical iPhone acceptance is separate from browser emulation.
